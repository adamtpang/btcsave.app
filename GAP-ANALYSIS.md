# Gap analysis: Iron vs JPMorgan Chase

One sentence: JPMorgan Chase is the scale case Iron's own roadmap names as its endpoint ("The limit: the credit and banking layer of a hyperbitcoinized world. Visa-plus-JPMorgan scale," MASTERPLAN.md section 7), so this measures Iron against Chase directly rather than against an arbitrary aspirational comparison.

## The benchmark

- **What:** JPMorgan Chase, and corroborated as Iron's own stated roadmap end-state, not just an external name-drop. Iron's MASTERPLAN.md section 7 ("Roadmap to the limit") ends its own four-stage roadmap (V1, Data, Graduate, Bank) with "The limit," described in the file's own words as Visa-plus-JPMorgan scale.
- **Why this is the right yardstick:** Chase is the lineage Iron's design already studies directly. `LEGENDS.md` grades Iron against Chase's own structural trick (a founding loophole, then a century of consolidation into a balance-sheet backstop), and Iron's graveyard rules exist specifically because Chase's ancestors (and rivals like Amex, Visa, Capital One) survived structural failures Iron is designed to avoid.
- **Source:** MASTERPLAN.md section 0 and section 7 (Iron's own citations); JPMorgan Chase total assets and market capitalization verified live, August 2026 (companiesmarketcap.com, stockanalysis.com, tradingeconomics.com): total assets approximately $4.9T, market capitalization approximately $950B to $1T depending on source and exact date. Note this is materially higher than the "~$837B" figure MASTERPLAN.md cites internally; that figure is stale and worth updating in the source file.

**Distance: 0/7 dimensions green.** One dimension (product breadth) is deliberately amber by design, not a gap to close early. The other six are red. This is stated plainly because it is true, not to be discouraging: Iron is pre-seed, pre-incorporation, pre-license, pre-revenue, exactly what "pre-seed" means. The value of this analysis is naming which of the six red dimensions is actually movable this week versus which are gated on capital or time.
